2026-07-09 EU Chat Control and the Erosion of Digital Freedom
NEWS

Every person in the member states of the EU is currently witnessing the greatest abuse of state power in decades, if not centuries.
I am talking about chat control.

It is a form of state arbitrariness and control that, in almost every country, runs counter to the rights of the population.
In Germany, the secrecy of correspondence is enshrined in Article 10 of the Basic Law (Grundgesetz).
In Austria: Article 10 of the Basic Law (Staatsgrundgesetz).
In Spain: Spanish Constitution, Article 18, paragraph 3.
The same applies, as it is anchored in the constitutions of almost all other countries (especially in Europe).

But for what purpose?
The official justification is that it is intended to prevent cyber-grooming and protect minors on the internet. Likewise, it is meant to uncover and prevent illegal activities prematurely.
But who will be the first to switch apps as soon as they are monitored? The criminals. They will not be successfully monitored. They use VPNs, switch to even more secure services, or use completely anonymous communication. They can set up their own servers. And yes, they can also lure their victims to their own services.
It would be naive to believe that our esteemed parliament is not aware of this, or at least suspicious of it. I submit that they know exactly that they are monitoring their people and depriving them of their freedom.
That it is a deliberate plan to be able to exert more state control.

Personally, I like to compare the situation to a panopticon: conformist behavior is achieved through the constant feeling of surveillance. In a panopticon, a guard can see into every cell, while the prisoner can never be sure whether they are currently being watched.
With chat control, every message can be read, processed, and stored. The individual never knows whether an investigation has already been launched against them, or whether the server containing their data has already been hacked and fallen into the hands of criminals.
There is a high risk of human error—be it unintentional or intentional (e.g., through bribery).

If I know that a house search is pending because I called my esteemed former Minister of Economic Affairs (Robert Habeck) a "moron" (Schwachkopf) in a repost on X—would I really still express myself critically and perhaps a bit bluntly in an actually private exchange with a friend about politicians or other powerful people?
To bring the comparison into the physical world: what would one talk about at the regulars' table (Stammtisch) if a police officer on duty were sitting there, ready to report you for the slightest wrong word?

It seems quite grim.
Why should one assume that politicians want exactly this?
One certainly shouldn't.
But it is possible. If the government becomes even more authoritarian at some point, such a system will be absolutely prone to abuse. And at that point, it will no longer be possible to stop it.
Today, we can still do exactly that. At least in theory.

It is important that we do not just treat symptoms. Obviously, I could advertise my own platform, Vincere—that everyone could set up their own version on their own servers, completely independently of me. But that would be symptom treatment.
Much more important is educating the population. Advocating for good education in schools. Teaching critical thinking to our own children. Actively consuming information, even in old age.

Personally, I see the goal we should work towards as a society where politicians play a less central role. And politicians must be accessible to the population.
Honestly: who knows who sits in Brussels and how to express their opinion to them? Local politicians with an office in one's own constituency are much closer to the people.
Furthermore, I am definitely in favor of referendums regarding any legislative drafts that restrict, change, or affect fundamental rights.
The first step for any person should be to be clear about the direction in which they are channeling their energy. If the goal is not known, one will wander aimlessly.
The next step is to find the right actions to implement what one envisions.
While doing so, one must consider how many compromises one is willing to make in order to act together with others.

2026-03-17 Vincere Roadmap & Desktop Client Architecture
PERSONAL

I am stk and the founder and lead developer of Vincere.
Vincere is a chat platform on the Tor network that prioritizes the highest possible level of privacy and anonymity.

The following tasks are planned for the near future:

  • Final bug fixes and release of the new update with group chat rooms
  • Implementation of a random one-to-one chat function
  • Development of the desktop version of the platform

One question has surely arisen: Why do we need a desktop version?

Good question! Currently, due to the blocked JavaScript in the Tor Browser, private key generation takes place on the server. This presents a security vulnerability. In theory, anyone with physical access to the server could intercept the keys (although this is quite difficult; I myself would hardly have the patience for it) and use them to decrypt end-to-end encrypted messages. (I'm bad at marketing, I'm too honest about this kind of thing...)

In the desktop version, key generation will take place directly on the end user's PC. Only the public key will still be visible to the server.

Now comes the next clever question: Why didn't you develop the messenger directly as a desktop application or on the clearnet (usually without JavaScript blocking)?

Quite simply: The darknet protects a lot of metadata. It allows for anonymity. Even if an attacker had access to the keys, they would see messages, but would hardly be able to trace who wrote them.
Anyone familiar with the site knows that there are no accounts and you can simply choose any nickname.
So, they could read quite a bit, but as long as you don't reveal your own address or names there, they would have hardly any useful information about you.
On the clearnet or in the desktop version, metadata can be transmitted more easily. Even if the content is encrypted, it would be possible to find out who is writing to whom, when, and from where.

I will therefore try to configure this as much as possible, including in the desktop version. The user can decide for themselves what is more important to them, or rather, use different platforms for different information.
